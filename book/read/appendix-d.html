64 CE

Nero used Christians as scapegoats after the Great Fire of Rome; Tacitus records punishments under Nero.

250 CE

Under Decius, empire-wide sacrifice was pressed as a test of loyalty.

303 CE

Under Diocletian, the last and most severe imperial persecution of Christians began.

313 CE

Constantine and Licinius issued the Edict of Milan, granting legal protection and return of confiscated Christian property.

325 CE

Constantine called the Council of Nicaea to address division, especially the Arian controversy over Christ's divine status and relationship to the Father.

After Nicaea

Constantine ordered Arius's writings burned.

367 CE

Athanasius's Thirty-Ninth Festal Letter listed the twenty-seven New Testament books recognized today and warned against apocryphal writings associated with heretics.

380 CE

The Edict of Thessalonica threatened divine punishment and imperial retribution for those who rejected the Nicene creed and helped establish Nicene Christianity as the state-backed orthodoxy of the Roman Empire.

1945 CE

The Nag Hammadi Library was discovered near Nag Hammadi in upper Egypt.
