The Body Must Become What It Proclaims

The Body Was Meant to Be Alive.

Riches are not righteousness. Noise is not power. A full building is not proof of a living body. Judge the tree by its fruit—love, mercy, truth, justice, service, freedom, and food in hungry hands.

Begin Chapter Twelve

◆

Complete Public Edition. This chapter is published in the complete online reader. Documented corrections, fuller context, and stronger supporting evidence remain welcome.

Why does so much of the church feel spiritually bankrupt?

Not every believer.

Not every gathering.

Not every pastor.

Not every church.

But the system.

The machine.

The thing that kept the name but lost the life.

The thing that kept the language of Jesus but lost the love of Jesus.

The thing that kept the building but lost the body.

That is the question.

Why does the body not operate in love and power?

Why are so many hungry while so many buildings sit full?

Why are so many lonely while so many people sing about family?

Why are so many wounded while so many pulpits talk about healing?

Why are so many afraid while so many claim to know perfect love?

Why are so many still begging outside the door when Jesus already opened the way?

Something is wrong.

And pretending it is not wrong does not make it holy.

Jesus said the world would know His disciples by their love. [John 13:35]

Not by their branding.

Not by their buildings.

Not by their signs.

Not by their conferences.

Not by their social media pages.

Not by their ability to quote verses while stepping over hungry people.

By love.

That is the test.

And Paul made it even sharper.

He said a person could speak with tongues, understand mysteries, have knowledge, and even have faith strong enough to move mountains — but without love, he is nothing. [1 Corinthians 13:1–2]

Nothing.

That should shake the costume.

Because it means a church can sound spiritual and still be empty.

A man can preach truth and still miss love.

A ministry can look powerful and still be bankrupt.

A body can have activity without life.

Noise without fruit.

Doctrine without mercy.

Knowledge without love.

Power talk without power.

Faith language without faith that actually feeds anyone.

That is spiritual bankruptcy.

Not having nothing.

Having everything except the one thing that proves life is there.

Love.

The danger is not just atheism outside the building.

The danger is dead religion inside it.

The danger is a body with no heartbeat.

The danger is a church with a stage but no table.

The danger is a room full of songs and a parking lot full of hungry people.

The danger is a people who know how to say “Lord” but forgot how to love their neighbor.

Jesus already warned us.

Not everyone who says, “Lord, Lord,” is known by Him. [Matthew 7:21–23]

That is terrifying.

Not because God is cruel.

Because religious performance can look alive while being dead.

Paul warned about people having a form of godliness but denying the power. [2 Timothy 3:5]

A form.

A shape.

A costume.

The outside appearance of faith.

But no life moving through it.

No power.

No love.

No mercy.

No freedom.

No courage.

No fruit.

And Jesus said we would know the tree by its fruit. [Matthew 7:16–20]

Not by its sign.

Not by its title.

Not by its size.

Not by its sound system.

Not by its budget.

Not by its history.

Not by its costume.

By its fruit.

And let us be clear.

Fruit is not money.

Fruit is not worldly prosperity.

Fruit is not the size of the building.

Fruit is not the number of people in the seats.

Fruit is not the reach of the platform.

Fruit is not the budget.

Fruit is not the brand.

Fruit is not how polished the machine looks from the outside.

Riches are not righteousness.

Success is not holiness.

A full room does not prove a living body.

A rich ministry does not prove a clean heart.

A popular preacher does not prove a true witness.

A wealthy church does not prove God is pleased.

If money were the proof of righteousness, Jesus would not have warned the rich man the way He did.

Jesus said it is hard for a rich man to enter the Kingdom of heaven.

Then He said it is easier for a camel to go through the eye of a needle than for a rich man to enter the Kingdom of God. [Matthew 19:23–24; Mark 10:23–25; Luke 18:24–25]

That should silence the prosperity costume.

Jesus did not say riches automatically prove blessing.

He warned that riches can become a trap.

Paul said the love of money is the root of all kinds of evil, and that some have pierced themselves through with many sorrows because of it. [1 Timothy 6:10]

So stop calling greed fruit.

Stop calling wealth anointing.

Stop calling popularity proof.

Stop calling expansion obedience.

Stop calling a bigger building revival.

Stop calling worldly success the blessing of God.

Fruit is not what the machine can count.

Fruit is what the Spirit produces.

Love.

Joy.

Peace.

Longsuffering.

Gentleness.

Goodness.

Faith.

Meekness.

Temperance. [Galatians 5:22–23]

That is fruit.

Mercy is fruit.

Humility is fruit.

Truth is fruit.

Freedom is fruit.

Compassion is fruit.

Justice is fruit.

Faithfulness is fruit.

Feeding the hungry is fruit.

Clothing the naked is fruit.

Visiting the forgotten is fruit.

Caring for the wounded is fruit.

Forgiving when pride wants revenge is fruit.

Correcting without crushing is fruit.

Serving without needing applause is fruit.

Showing up when nobody is filming is fruit.

That is how we judge the tree.

Not by gold on the walls.

Not by numbers on a screen.

Not by the preacher’s car.

Not by the size of the offering.

Not by how many people know the name.

By fruit.

Jesus said the tree is known by its fruit. [Matthew 7:16–20]

And in Matthew 25, the dividing line was not who had the biggest ministry.

It was who fed the hungry.

Who gave drink to the thirsty.

Who welcomed the stranger.

Who clothed the naked.

Who visited the sick and imprisoned. [Matthew 25:35–40]

That is the fruit Jesus named.

So if a church is rich but the hungry stay hungry, do not call that fruit.

If a ministry is famous but the wounded are stepped over, do not call that fruit.

If a preacher is wealthy but people are kept afraid, ashamed, and dependent, do not call that fruit.

If a system grows while love dies, do not call that fruit.

That is not Kingdom.

That is empire with worship music.

So let me make this plain.

There is a woman who comes by the hotel at least five nights a week.

She buys the food herself.

She cooks it herself.

She drives through and feeds whoever is hungry.

She makes plates for people who may not have eaten otherwise.

No stage.

No spotlight.

No church banner.

No food bank partnership.

No microphone.

No offering bucket.

No religious announcement.

Just obedience.

Just love.

Just a woman feeding hungry people because she loves God and she loves His children.

That is fruit.

That is the body working.

That is what love looks like when it stops talking and starts moving.

Jesus said, “Feed my sheep.” [John 21:15–17]

She is doing it.

Jesus said, “I was an hungred, and ye gave me meat.” [Matthew 25:35]

She is doing it.

James said pure religion is to care for the vulnerable in their affliction and to keep oneself clean from the world. [James 1:27]

She is closer to that than many buildings with crosses on them.

And that should make us honest.

Because if a woman with no platform, no program, no official backing, and no religious spotlight can feed hungry people five nights a week, then maybe the problem was never that the body lacked resources.

Maybe the problem is that the body forgot what it was for.

Maybe the problem is not that love is impossible.

Maybe the problem is that love became optional.

Maybe the problem is that service became a department instead of a way of life.

Maybe the problem is that mercy became a ministry title instead of food in someone’s hands.

That woman does not need a pulpit to preach.

Her life is already speaking.

She does not need a title to serve.

Her fruit already testifies.

She does not need a church program to obey God.

She heard the Father and moved.

That is love with legs on it.

That is faith with food in its hands.

That is power without performance.

And that is why the machine should tremble.

Because while some argue doctrine with empty hands, she is feeding Christ in the hungry.

While some defend the costume, she is living the Kingdom.

While some ask who authorized her, heaven already sees the fruit.

While some build platforms, she is building plates.

While some talk about revival, she is keeping somebody alive long enough to see tomorrow.

Judge the tree by its fruit.

Her fruit is love.

Her fruit is mercy.

Her fruit is obedience.

Her fruit is food in the hands of hungry people.

Her fruit is movement.

Her fruit is care.

Her fruit is doing what many only preach about.

And I tell you plainly:

God sees that.

God sees the hidden meal.

God sees the money spent.

God sees the time given.

God sees the tired hands still serving.

God sees the plates made in love.

God sees the hungry fed when nobody was filming.

God sees the servant nobody gave a title to.

And if men want to talk about calling, let the fruit speak.

If men want to talk about anointing, let the hungry testify.

If men want to talk about authority, let mercy answer.

Because love carries authority that titles cannot fake.

The body was never meant to be a religious audience.

The body was meant to move.

Hands feeding.

Feet going.

Mouths speaking truth.

Arms carrying the weak.

Eyes seeing the forgotten.

Hearts refusing to become numb.

That is body.

A body that does not move is sick.

A body that does not feel is numb.

A body that does not feed is failing.

A body that does not love is not operating from the life of Christ.

This is why so much of the church feels powerless.

Because power was never given so people could perform.

Power was given so people could witness.

Heal.

Serve.

Love.

Tell the truth.

Cast out fear.

Lift the broken.

Feed the hungry.

Set captives free.

Jesus did not give His people life so they could build a religious brand.

He gave them life so they could become a living witness.

The early body understood this.

They continued in teaching, fellowship, breaking bread, and prayer.

They shared.

They gave.

They ate together.

They cared for needs.

They moved with gladness and singleness of heart. [Acts 2:42–47]

They were of one heart and one soul, and great grace was upon them. [Acts 4:32–33]

That was body.

Not business.

Not branding.

Not empire.

Body.

A living people.

A shared life.

A witness with love and power.

But when the body becomes a business, people become customers.

When the body becomes a stage, people become spectators.

When the body becomes a machine, people become parts.

When the body becomes a hierarchy, sons and daughters become servants of the system.

When the body becomes a costume, power leaves and performance remains.

That is why the warning to Laodicea matters.

Jesus said:

“I know thy works, that thou art neither cold nor hot: I would thou wert cold or hot.”

Then He said:

“Because thou art lukewarm, and neither cold nor hot, I will spue thee out of my mouth.” [Revelation 3:15–16]

That is not soft.

That is not decoration.

That is danger.

The lukewarm church was not warned because it had no activity.

It had works.

It had confidence.

It had resources.

It said, “I am rich, and increased with goods, and have need of nothing.”

But Jesus said it was wretched, miserable, poor, blind, and naked. [Revelation 3:17]

That is spiritual bankruptcy.

Having money but lacking mercy.

Having confidence but lacking sight.

Having religion but lacking reality.

Having works but lacking fire.

Having a name but lacking truth.

Having a form but denying the power.

So the question is not, “Can the church survive?”

The question is:

Will the church wake up before it becomes useless to the One it claims to serve?

Will it return to love?

Will it return to truth?

Will it return to humility?

Will it return to the Father?

Will it return to the Spirit?

Will it return to the Christ who stands at the door and knocks? [Revelation 3:20]

Because the danger is plain.

A church can be busy and still be bankrupt.

A church can be rich and still be poor.

A church can be loud and still be dead.

A church can quote Scripture and still resist the Spirit.

A church can claim Jesus and still refuse His way.

A church can call itself body and still not operate in love.

And if the body does not operate in love, it is not functioning as His body.

If it does not operate in truth, it is not walking in His light.

If it does not operate in freedom, it is not showing His Spirit.

If it does not operate in mercy, it is not reflecting His Father.

If it does not operate in power, it is not living from His life.

So where is the fruit?

Where is the love?

Where is the mercy?

Where is the plate of food?

Where is the hand on the shoulder?

Where is the visit?

Where is the prayer that does not perform?

Where is the truth that does not crush?

Where is the correction that restores?

Where is the table?

Where is the family?

Where is the body?

The church should not need a program to notice hungry people.

The church should not need a committee to love its neighbor.

The church should not need a brand strategy to obey Jesus.

The church should not need applause to serve.

The church should not need a camera to feed someone.

The church should not need permission from the machine to move in love.

If love is real, it moves.

If faith is alive, it works.

If the Spirit is present, fruit grows.

And if there is no fruit, we need to stop defending the tree.

This is not condemnation.

This is warning.

This is mercy telling the truth before the collapse.

Because Jesus did not warn Laodicea because He hated them.

He warned them because they were blind and did not know it.

That is mercy.

Hard mercy.

Clear mercy.

The kind of mercy that tells the truth while there is still time to wake up.

So hear the warning.

If we keep the name but lose the love, we are in danger.

If we keep the book but miss the One, we are in danger.

If we keep the form but deny the power, we are in danger.

If we keep the worship but ignore the wounded, we are in danger.

If we keep the doctrine but lose the Father’s heart, we are in danger.

If we keep the church but lose the body, we are in danger.

The costume cannot heal anyone.

The machine cannot raise the dead.

The shelf cannot love your neighbor.

The brand cannot cast out fear.

The system cannot replace the Spirit.

And dead religion cannot carry the living Christ.

So let the veil tear here too.

Tear the veil off the powerless church.

Tear the veil off religious performance.

Tear the veil off empty activity.

Tear the veil off loveless doctrine.

Tear the veil off prosperity pretending to be righteousness.

Tear the veil off greed dressed up as blessing.

Tear the veil off the lie that money proves God is pleased.

Tear the veil off the lie that a building is alive just because people gather in it.

Tear the veil off the idea that God is impressed by noise without love.

Tear the veil off the comfort that says, “We have need of nothing,” while Jesus says we are poor, blind, and naked.

The body was meant to be alive.

Alive with love.

Alive with truth.

Alive with mercy.

Alive with courage.

Alive with power.

Alive with the Spirit.

Alive enough to see hunger and move.

Alive enough to see pain and care.

Alive enough to hear the Father and obey.

Alive enough to feed His sheep.

So if you want to know where God is moving, do not only look at the stage.

Look for the fruit.

Look for love when nobody is filming.

Look for mercy when nobody is clapping.

Look for food handed to hungry people.

Look for the servant who keeps showing up.

Look for the person who does not need permission to obey God.

Look for the tree.

Then judge it by its fruit.

Because the body was never meant to be spiritually bankrupt.

The body was meant to be alive.

And love is the proof.

Chapter Connections

Fruit, Service and a Living Body

Chapter Twelve rejects money, popularity, platforms, expansion, and polished appearances as proof of righteousness. The measure is living fruit.

Wealth, attendance, buildings, branding, and reach are not spiritual fruit. The chapter measures fruit by love, mercy, truth, justice, humility, and faithfulness.

Living Witness

Faith With Food

The woman feeding hungry people at the hotel becomes the chapter’s concrete picture of obedience: service without stage, title, applause, or camera.

Final Warning

Wake Up, Body

The warning to Laodicea exposes the danger of having resources, confidence, activity, and religion while remaining poor in love, sight, mercy, and living power.
